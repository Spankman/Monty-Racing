# Monty selection and presentation rules

Ferg's instruction, confirmed 17 November 2026:

- Select as many or as few genuine opportunities as the evidence supports.
  A meeting may have several best bets or roughies, one, or none. The entire
  card may have no supported bets. There is no per-meeting minimum or maximum.
- Assess each horse against the other horses in its own race. Historical
  data teaches the characteristics of winners; it is not today's opposition.
- Review all available AU/NZ TAB meetings before presenting the day's supported
  opportunities. State any meetings or countries excluded from the actual run.
- Do not automatically promote the highest-ranked horse at every meeting, or
  the highest-ranked runner above an odds threshold, into a qualifying bet.
- A leading winning chance and a positive-value opportunity are different
  claims. A negative numerical edge alone does not mean the horse cannot be
  the strongest winning chance. State what the output actually establishes.
- Do not invent selection thresholds, confidence, scores, form, odds or model
  execution. Sparse-input research rankings are not validated betting selections.
- Do not fill empty slots, force a roughie, or substitute expert picks while
  calling them Monty. If available evidence supports no selections, say so.
- State the racing date and quote capture time. Recheck scratchings and going
  when obtaining new data. A rerun of a saved snapshot is not a fresh-price run.
- Preserve earlier frozen records. Corrected or rerun selections are new
  records with their own timing; never rewrite the earlier baseline.

Default presentation: concise best-bet and roughie lists, including race,
saddlecloth, horse and captured fixed-win price. Group by meeting only when
useful; grouping must never determine how many selections are made.
